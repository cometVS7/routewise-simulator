The Core Analogy

Packets on the Highway: Forwarding vs. Routing

When you send a request from your laptop in Pune to a cloud server, your data is sliced into packets. Think of each packet as a car driving across a national expressway network where routers are highway intersections.

🚦 Forwarding (The Data Plane)

The fast, local action at a single junction. A packet arrives, the router checks its local signpost (Forwarding Table), and pushes the packet out the right exit ramp.

🗺️ Routing (The Control Plane)

The network-wide intelligence that builds those signposts in the first place. Routers talk to each other using Routing Algorithms to discover the best end-to-end paths.

🚗 IP Packet A car carrying data toward a destination address
🔀 Router Highway interchange deciding which exit to take
🛣️ Link Cost Toll fee, road congestion, or inverse bandwidth
🧭 Routing Table The highway signboards telling cars the next hop

Algorithm 01 · Distance-Vector ("Routing by Rumor")

Asking Officers at Each Junction

Imagine driving without a map. At each junction, you ask the local traffic officer how far your destination is. The officer doesn't have a state map either, he only knows the distance to his immediate neighbor junctions and what those neighbors told him. Every router repeatedly applies the Bellman-Ford Equation:

Dₓ(y) = minᵥ { c(x,v) + Dᵥ(y) } Cost to neighbour v + neighbour v's rumoured distance to y

Algorithm 02 · Link-State ("The Live Satellite GPS")

Full Map First, Shortest Path Second

Instead of trading rumors at junctions, every router broadcasts a Link-State Advertisement (LSA) listing its direct neighbors and link costs to every router in the network. Once every router has the exact same complete map (LSDB), it runs Dijkstra’s Shortest Path First (SPF) algorithm:

1. Flood LSAs → 2. Build LSDB → 3. Run Dijkstra SPF Repeatedly finalize the closest unvisited node & relax its outgoing links

Protocol Deep Dive

How RIP and OSPF Work in Real Networks

1. RIP (Routing Information Protocol) — The Junction Counter

• Algorithm: Distance-Vector (Bellman-Ford).
• Metric: Pure Hop Count (every router = 1 hop). Maximum valid path is 15 hops; 16 means unreachable (infinity) to cap count-to-infinity loops.
• Updates: Routers broadcast their full routing table to immediate neighbors every 30 seconds over UDP port 520.
• Limitation: Completely blind to bandwidth—it prefers 2 hops over slow copper to 3 hops over 10 Gbps fiber.

2. OSPF (Open Shortest Path First) — The Smart City GPS

• Algorithm: Link-State (floods LSAs, builds an LSDB, runs Dijkstra's SPF).
• Metric: Link cost inversely proportional to bandwidth (e.g., 10 Gbps fiber has a much lower cost than 100 Mbps Ethernet).
• Hierarchical Scaling with Areas: To stop LSA flooding from overwhelming huge networks, OSPF divides an organization into smaller Areas connected through a central hub called Backbone Area 0.

💡 Key Exam Insight Both RIP and OSPF are Interior Gateway Protocols (IGPs)—they only route traffic inside a single organization's Autonomous System (AS).
Inter-AS Routing · Connecting the Whole Internet

BGP (Border Gateway Protocol): International Customs & Air Traffic Control

Why can’t OSPF or RIP just run across the entire global Internet? Two reasons: Scale (millions of routers would drown in flooded LSAs) and Privacy & Policy (ISPs like Jio, Airtel, and cloud giants like AWS or Netflix do not want competitors seeing their internal router maps or forcing traffic through their cables for free).

To solve this, the Internet is divided into independent kingdoms called Autonomous Systems (AS), each with a unique number (ASN). Inside an AS, organizations run RIP or OSPF. Between different ASes, the entire world uses one protocol: BGP.

Concept 01 · Path-Vector

Passport Stamps (AS-PATH)

Instead of advertising link costs, BGP stamps every route advertisement with the list of Autonomous Systems it has crossed (e.g., AS 200 → AS 300). Just like a customs officer checking passport stamps, if a border router sees its own AS number already in the list, it immediately rejects the route—stopping loops effortlessly!

Concept 02 · Policy First

Diplomacy Over Raw Speed

Imagine an airline choosing international flight paths. Even if flying over Country X is 20 minutes shorter, the airline won't fly there if they don't have a treaty or if transit fees are too high. Similarly, BGP chooses routes based on business contracts (Customer, Peer, Provider) first, and uses path length only as a tie-breaker.

Concept 03 · eBGP vs. iBGP

Border Gate vs. Internal Memo

BGP runs over TCP port 179. Border routers use eBGP (External BGP) to learn routes from neighboring countries (other ASes), and iBGP (Internal BGP) to distribute those international reachability memos to routers inside their own AS.

Real-World Case Study A

Tracing One Packet: From Pune Campus to Netflix / AWS

  1. Inside Campus (OSPF): Your laptop sends a packet to your campus default gateway. Campus routers use OSPF to zip the packet along fast internal fiber to the Campus Border Router.
  2. Crossing to the ISP (eBGP Handoff): OSPF ends at the campus gate. The border router checks its BGP table, picks the approved AS-PATH to the cloud provider, and hands the packet to the ISP.
  3. Inside the Cloud (OSPF / IS-IS): After crossing ISP border links via eBGP, the packet enters the cloud data center’s AS, where internal Link-State routing carries it to the final web server.
Real-World Case Study B

The 2021 Facebook Global BGP Outage

On October 4, 2021, Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp vanished from the Internet for 6 hours. Their servers weren't hacked—a maintenance command accidentally triggered a withdrawal of Facebook's BGP route advertisements.

In our highway analogy: All of Facebook's cities, roads, and buildings were completely fine inside, but they accidentally erased their country from every international border map. No router on Earth knew which highway exit led to Facebook!

Exam Revision

5 Key Takeaways to Remember

  1. Forwarding vs. Routing: Forwarding moves a packet across a single router using a table (Data Plane); Routing calculates the paths that build that table (Control Plane).
  2. Distance-Vector (Bellman-Ford): Routers exchange distance estimates with direct neighbors only. Simple overhead, but bad news travels slowly (fixed via Split Horizon).
  3. Link-State (Dijkstra): Routers flood LSAs to build an identical Link-State Database (LSDB) map and compute shortest-path trees independently.
  4. RIP vs. OSPF: RIP counts hops (max 15) and ignores link speed; OSPF weights links by bandwidth and scales using Areas connected to Area 0.
  5. BGP Glues the Internet Together: Connects independent Autonomous Systems (AS), prevents loops using AS-PATH, and puts business policy ahead of raw speed.
